Syl Foisy

Hi! I'm a PhD candidate at NASA GISS and Columbia University studying aerosols, the tiny liquid and solid particles in the atmosphere that have an outsized influence on our climate, our weather, and the air we breathe.

Outside of research, I have many interests, ranging from filmmaking to snow sculpture, which occasionally, and delightfully, collide with my science.

I research
the sky

My research interests are all things aerosols and clouds. Currently, I use satellite data from NASA's PACE mission and NASA GISS ModelE climate model simulations to study the evolution of aerosol microphysical properties, and how they influence cloud formation. I'm advised by Dr. Susanne Bauer and Dr. Kostas Tsigaridis.

I love trying
new things

Before starting my PhD, I built a cloud chamber at Williams College in collaboration with Prof. Anthony Carrasquillo and Prof. Miriam Freedman to study how aerosols respond to changes in humidity. Many undergo a striking transformation, separating into two distinct liquid phases much like oil and water, with real consequences for how aerosols scatter light, facilitate cloud formation, and shape climate. I'm proud that students at Williams continue this research today.

Scientist interviews

Hi, I’m Syl, an Earth Science PhD candidate! From May 23 to June 3, 2026, I walked 250 miles from NYC to DC as a co-organizer of the Weather and Climate Livestream, interviewing 14 weather and climate experts along the way. The walk was inspired by federal budget cuts threatening the science behind our disaster forecasts, our planting seasons, and our plans for a changing climate. I was blown away when 300 letters arrived from people who shared those concerns, and I was lucky enough to hand-deliver these letters to seven members of Congress at a press conference in DC upon my arrival. I want to thank Miriam Nielsen and Virginia Schutte for helping film the interviews, and for supporting me throughout the walk!
